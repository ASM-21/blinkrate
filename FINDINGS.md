# Findings

One file, every result, with its caveat attached. Numbers are regenerated by
`python -m blinkrate.run_all` and checked against this text by
`python tools/check_claims.py`, which exits non-zero on drift.

Sample: 3,427 utility-state-years from EIA-861 reliability filings for 2013,
2014, 2015 and 2022. Not a continuous panel — see `docs/DATA_ACCESS.md` for why
the middle years are missing and what that costs.

---

## 1. The threshold parameter moves reported reliability more than most utility
programs do

Under IEEE 1366's own log-normal assumption, re-evaluating the same filed outage
records at different values of the major-event-day threshold parameter beta
gives a customer-weighted fleet SAIDI of:

| beta | fleet SAIDI without MED | change | expected qualifying days/yr |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.5 | 39 min/yr | -66% | 24.4 |
| 2.0 | 68 min/yr | -40% | 8.3 |
| **2.5 (the standard)** | **114 min/yr** | — | **2.27 qualifying days** |
| 3.0 | 259 min/yr | +127% | 0.5 |

A **6.62x** range, on 2,267 utility-years whose reported numbers the model can
represent, with no change to any underlying outage.

The standard's value of 2.5 sits close to where the mechanism stops working.
Under log-normality the median utility-year contains no qualifying day at all
once beta passes about 2.95 — 0.45 above the published value.

**Caveat that changes how much this is worth.** This is a model-implied
sensitivity, not a measurement. EIA-861 publishes annual aggregates, never daily
SAIDI, so no threshold can be recomputed directly; the sweep works by inverting
the closed-form exclusion identity (`docs/derivation.md`) to recover the one
latent parameter. Track 8 attack A1 re-runs it with heavier-tailed daily SAIDI:
the direction survives everywhere, the magnitude shrinks by up to 3.05x, and the
cliff at 2.95 disappears entirely. **The direction is a property of the rule.
The magnitude is a property of the log-normal.**

## 2. Nearly a third of filings report no major event days at all

**30.3% of filings** report a retained share of exactly 1.00 — SAIDI without
major event days identical to SAIDI with them.

Under the standard's own log-normal model this should be rare. A perfectly
uniform utility, at zero daily dispersion, still excludes 0.6% of its minutes,
because a continuous distribution always puts some mass above a finite
threshold. Only **66.2%** of filings fall inside what the model can produce;
31.3% are too clean and 2.6% exclude more than any dispersion generates.

A Chetty-style excess-mass estimator, with a fifth-degree counterfactual fitted
outside the bunching window and bootstrapped over utility-state clusters, puts
the mass at **9.3x** the counterfactual density (95% CI 5.7 to 23.6). It survives
32 of 36 alternative specifications; the four failures all widen the window to
0.10 with a flexible polynomial (attack A3).

**What this is not.** It is not evidence that anyone is working the rule to their
advantage, and reporting zero exclusion
is not flattering — it makes a utility's headline number worse, not better. The
likeliest reading is that a large minority of utilities do not apply the
major-event-day methodology at all. That is still a comparability problem: a
national SAIDI average pools utilities using two different measurement rules.

## 3. Most of the decade's reported deterioration is in the excluded part

On a balanced panel of **589 utility-states** present in all four filing years,
2013 to 2022:

- SAIDI with major event days: +65.6%
- SAIDI without major event days: +22.7%
- the gap between them: +105.0%

Utility by utility, so composition cannot drive it, **82.9% of the** increase in
reported with-MED SAIDI comes from the excluded part rather than from everyday
outage minutes. Trimming extreme utilities gives 78.7%; dropping customer
weighting gives 81.1% (attack A4).

Alongside that, a net 8.3 points of utilities moved into using the exclusion:
19.2% reported zero exclusion in 2013 and some in 2022, while 10.9% went the
other way.

**Hard limit.** Without an external weather severity index this track cannot
separate "more severe weather" from "more days classified as major events" —
which is exactly the distinction it was scoped to make. NOAA is blocked and no
mirrored storm panel keyed to service territories was found. Read this as
bounding the question, not settling it. The methodology-adoption movement is at
least partly a classification story, since switching from reporting no exclusion
to reporting some is a discrete methodology change rather than weather.

## 4. Who excludes: ownership matters, but less than the pooled numbers suggest

Median retained share: investor-owned **0.644**, cooperative 0.755, municipal
**0.926**. Municipals report zero exclusion in 46.7% of filings, IOUs in 13.0%.

Conditioning matters more than the raw gap. IOU status lowers the probability of
reporting zero exclusion by 18.2 points with no size control and **7.5 points**
once log customers, state and year are controlled (p = 0.026). Restricting to
utilities above median size shrinks the median-retained-share gap from 0.111 to
0.038 (attack A5).

So roughly two thirds of the apparent ownership effect is size. Something
survives, and it is consistent with the regulatory-scrutiny story, but it is also
consistent with larger utilities simply having outage management systems capable
of classifying days. This data cannot separate those.

The reporting standard is discretionary too. Utilities filing under a
self-defined "Other" standard rather than IEEE 1366 report a median retained
share of 0.878 against 0.713, and report zero exclusion 40.0% of the time against
26.2%. 14.6% of utility-states switched standard at least once across the four
years.

## 5. Cause-specific exclusions stack, and nobody looks at the second one

89.9% of IEEE-standard filers also report a loss-of-supply variant, removing
outages that originate upstream of distribution. It removes a median of 4.0% of
outage minutes (retained share **0.960**), 7.8% customer-weighted.

Stacked with the major-event-day exclusion, the median utility-year reports
59.3% of its raw outage minutes. Municipals mostly do not use it: their median
loss-of-supply retained share is 1.000 and 71.6% remove nothing.

## 6. Capital deployment: no detectable effect, and the design could not have
found one

AMI share carries a coefficient of **-0.185** on log SAIDI without major event
days in the 2022 cross-section (95% CI -0.417 to +0.048, n = 624, state and
ownership fixed effects, standard errors clustered by state). Voltage-optimized
circuit share is also indistinguishable from zero.

Attack A6 is the important part. The minimum detectable effect at 80% power is
0.332 log points, a 28.2% SAIDI change, while the point estimate corresponds to
about 17%. And 47.6% of utility-states are already at 100% AMI by 2022, with an
interquartile range of 0.96 to 1.00. **This is not evidence that metering
deployment does not help. It is evidence that a 2022 cross-section, run after the
technology became near-universal, cannot tell.**

There is no spending variable anywhere in this repo: FERC Form 1 is unreachable
and has no GitHub mirror, so track 1 regresses physical deployment instead and
track 4's vegetation-management question is marked blocked rather than proxied.

A variance decomposition on the 2013-2015 balanced panel bounds what any
cross-sectional regressor could do: **74%** of log-SAIDI variance is a persistent
utility effect and 26% is year-to-year shock.

## 7. Undergrounding misses breakeven by an order of magnitude at average density

A payback model, not a measurement, with every parameter sourced inline in
`blinkrate/track5_undergrounding.py`.

At the national average of **30.5 customers per** line mile (167.8M customers
from EIA-861 2022 over 5.5M miles of distribution line), $3.8M per circuit mile,
$370.2 per customer-hour, 30-year life at 7%, and the *most favourable* published
reliability benefit (99.6% SAIDI reduction, Virginia Electric and Power 2025),
the baseline SAIDI required to break even is **1,633 min/yr**. The median 2022
utility reports 173.7. Only 2.5% of 2022 filings clear the bar.

Using the econometric estimate instead of the case studies moves the breakeven to
16,266 min/yr, which nothing reaches — a 7.3x swing driven purely by which
literature you believe. Engineering case studies of converted circuits report 73%
to 99.6% SAIDI reductions; the utility-level econometric estimate (Larsen et al.
2020) finds the SAIDI relationship statistically insignificant.

Density is what decides it. The same model at 400 customers per mile puts
breakeven at 125 min/yr, below the national median. Undergrounding pencils in
dense urban territory and does not at average density — which is consistent with
it being justified in practice on wildfire risk and aesthetics rather than on
avoided outage minutes.

## The thread across tracks

Every track lands on the same place from a different direction: **what gets
reported is decided more by measurement rules than by anything on the poles.**

- The threshold parameter moves fleet SAIDI 6.62x (track 3).
- Most of the decade's deterioration is in the part that gets excluded (track 2).
- A third of utilities do not apply the exclusion rule at all, and which standard
  they file under moves their number more than ownership does (tracks 3, 6).
- Two carve-outs stack to remove 41% of median outage minutes (track 4).
- The one thing that should move the number — capital deployment — cannot be
  detected, and the spending data that would test it properly is unreachable
  (track 1).

That is an argument for treating cross-utility SAIDI comparisons as weak
evidence, and for regulators specifying the measurement rule as tightly as they
specify the target.

## What would change these conclusions

- **Daily SAIDI series from any utility.** Would replace track 3's model-implied
  sweep with a measurement and settle attack A1 outright.
- **FERC Form 1.** Would give tracks 1 and 4 a spending variable.
- **The 2016-2021 filing years.** Would turn four points with a gap into a real
  panel and let track 2 date the change.
- **A storm severity index keyed to service territories.** Would let track 2
  split weather from classification, which is the question it exists to answer.
